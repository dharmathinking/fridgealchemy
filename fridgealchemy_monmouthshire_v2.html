Where this came from

An experienced cook.
No chef's hat.

FridgeAlchemy didn't start as a business idea. It started as a question — what's in the fridge? — asked on a Saturday afternoon when dinner hadn't been planned and the week's leftovers were looking interesting. A bit of this from Tuesday, something from last night, half a thing that hadn't made it to the dog yet.

My son Jack, who grew up eating this way, told me recently that he never thought about food any other way — that everything on a plate was always just different ingredients to him, not a finished thing to be received. That's what twenty years of a family kitchen does. And it's what FridgeAlchemy is trying to share.

No Michelin ambitions. No formal training. Twenty-one years running a consultancy, a lifetime fell running and foraging in the Welsh Borders, and the conviction that the landscape around us holds everything we need to eat well.

Between Abergavenny and Raglan, Monmouthshire

What this is actually about

FridgeAlchemy is not a showcase. The food is never the point on its own — it's the thing that makes the conversation happen, that gets people into a kitchen together, that connects what's on the plate to the landscape outside the window.

Some evenings guests are invited to send a photo of their fridge and larder before they arrive. The meal is partly built around what they already have at home. That's not a gimmick — it's the whole idea. Food as autobiography. The kitchen as a space for participation, not performance.

The community comes first. Everything else follows from that.

Kitchen

Supper evenings and hands-on workshops from a Monmouthshire kitchen. Guests cook alongside, not in front of. The meal comes from what's available — local, seasonal, honest. Gluten-free throughout.

The FridgeAlchemy Network

Become a FridgeAlchemist

The question — what's in the fridge? — works anywhere. In a kitchen in Monmouthshire, on a hillside somewhere, or at a table in your own home, wherever that is. If the idea resonates with how you already think about food and community, we'd like to help you build it where you are.

Your kitchen, your people

FridgeAlchemy events happen in real kitchens — domestic, unhurried, a little imperfect. Everything about how you run yours, including any commercial arrangements, is yours entirely.

Tools, not rules

We'll share what we've learned — event formats, hosting guides, the fridge-and-larder invitation, and the booklet template. That booklet — guests' three pictures, prompt questions, notes space, and the evening's recipes — is one of the most distinctive things FridgeAlchemy does. It travels with the idea.

A community of practice

Connect with others running FridgeAlchemy evenings in different places. Over time, the booklets from different kitchens become a distributed record of how the idea lives in different hands, different landscapes, different larders. That's the network.
